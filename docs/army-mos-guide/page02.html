Fast Lane vs. Slow Lane
Trusted Workforce 2.0 cut the routine timeline dramatically — but a foreign-contact flag still pulls a case out of the fast lane.
🟢 Routine Lane (TW2.0)

No disqualifying flags on automated checks → streamlined continuous-evaluation track → ~60-day median per current DCSA/Performance.gov targets. This is the new normal for most candidates post-reform.

🟠 Foreign-Contact Flagged Lane

Immediate family with foreign citizenship routinely triggers manual adjudicator review under Guideline B → case pulled from the fast queue → historically 6–18 months, sometimes longer, independent of the candidate's underlying loyalty.

6
Guideline B — What Triggers Scrutiny
Disqualifying Conditions (DC) — the mere existence of these facts opens a review, regardless of actual loyalty.

DC 1 — Exploitable Contact

Any contact with a foreign family member, associate, or friend that creates heightened risk of exploitation, inducement, or coercion.

DC 2 — Conflict of Interest

Ties to a foreign person, group, or government creating a conflict between protecting classified info and helping the foreign entity.

DC 3 — Shared Residence

Cohabitation with a person — any citizenship — if it creates heightened risk of foreign coercion.

7
Mitigating Conditions — The Candidate's Playbook
A triggered DC shifts the burden of proof — not a denial. These four conditions, aggressively documented, are what wins the case.

MC 1 — Nature of Contact

Prove parents aren't agents of a foreign power and aren't positioned to be exploited — occupation, politics, host-nation stability.

MC 2 — Casual & Infrequent

Establish the real frequency and nature of contact — benign, non-reliant communication reduces risk.

MC 3 — Full Disclosure

Report every foreign contact, travel, and financial interest on the SF-86 — no room for a concealment finding.

MC 4 — Financial Sovereignty

No reliance on foreign parents for support, no large foreign assets that could be held hostage — U.S.-rooted finances.
